Add rooms: a home for chats

Chats gave the conversation boundaries. A room gives those boundaries a
SUBJECT, and that is the part a flat list cannot express: a chat titled
from its first message tells AURA nothing about how to behave, while a
room can say "teach, don't just answer".

Built on top of chat_sessions rather than replacing it. A room is a
parent row in chat_rooms, and chat_sessions gains a nullable room_id, so
every chat that exists today keeps working exactly as it does now --
just unfiled until it is put somewhere. Nothing about the existing chat,
voice or history features changes.

  * Chats is a new sidebar destination: rooms on the left with their
    chats nested under them, the open chat's transcript on the right.
  * Entering a room resumes its most recent chat, or starts one there.
    A room is never a conversation of its own -- messages always live in
    a chat.
  * core/room_router.py scores each message against every room's
    keywords, name and topic and moves you when the subject changes,
    saying so as it does. A context change you cannot see is
    indistinguishable from AURA losing the thread. Nothing is hardcoded
    to "coding" or "Japanese"; those are rows, so a room invented
    tomorrow routes the same way. A wrong switch costs far more than a
    missed one, so a challenger must clear an absolute floor AND beat
    the current room by a margin.
  * build_context_prompt now carries the room's topic and house style,
    which is what stops her answering a kanji question like a code
    review.
  * Deleting a room keeps its chats, unfiled. Deleting a folder must
    never delete the conversations inside it.

Verified against the live database: created a room, entered it, wrote a
message, deleted the room, confirmed the chat survived. Routing places a
KeyError in Coding and a te-form question in Japanese Study, and leaves
"I'm reading the docs" where it was. tsc --noEmit clean.
